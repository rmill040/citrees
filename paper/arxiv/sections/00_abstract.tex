Decision trees that choose a feature and a threshold in one search favor
features with many possible thresholds. Conditional inference trees (CIT) and
forests (CIF) test features before choosing thresholds
to reduce this split selection bias, but their repeated permutation tests make
them costly. We compare CIT and CIF with 15
classification and 16 regression rankers, including random forests (RF) and RF
recursive feature elimination (RF-RFE), on 31 real
datasets, scoring each ranking by held-out balanced accuracy or $R^2$ of
fixed learners on its top-$k$ features, and on synthetic designs with
known informative features. CIF ranks tied 3rd of 17 in classification, level
with RF and behind RF-RFE and XGBoost, and 2nd of 18 in regression, behind
ExtraTrees. On smaller panels where every method completed it is tied 6th
and tied 5th, and the regression ordering is descriptive. It is at or below the
median on synthetic recovery (8th of 15, 11th of 16) and weakest at small
$k$ in classification. At the minimum permutation budget, the two Bonferroni corrections, in the
Stage~A feature test over $m$ features and the Stage~B threshold test over $K$
candidate thresholds, cost of order $m^2/\alpha$ and $K^2/\alpha$ permutations
per node. An opt-in max-type rule tests the thresholds jointly at the
nominal level with about $999K$ statistic evaluations, loses 0.006 in power at equal
realized size, and moves CIF to 5th of 17 and 4th of 18. The results
support CIF as a top-$k$ feature-ranking method for downstream prediction,
level with random forests in classification.
