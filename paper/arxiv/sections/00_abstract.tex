Conditional inference trees (CIT) and conditional inference forests (CIF)
reduce split-selection bias by testing features before choosing split
thresholds, but repeated permutation tests and threshold searches can make these
methods computationally expensive. We study CIT and CIF as top-$k$
feature-ranking methods for downstream prediction using real-data benchmarks,
runtime ablations, and synthetic feature-recovery experiments. Each split passes
a Stage~A feature test and a Stage~B threshold test, each under a Bonferroni
correction. At a fixed node, if the features and permutation budget do not
depend on the node responses, Bonferroni-corrected $+1$ Monte Carlo permutation
$P$-values control nodewise rejection under the complete permutation null.
We benchmark them against 15 classification and 16 regression rankers, among
them partykit's ctree and cforest, random forests (RF), recursive feature
elimination with random forests (RF-RFE), ExtraTrees, and gradient boosting, on
31 real datasets, scoring each ranking by the held-out accuracy of fixed
learners fitted to its top-$k$ features. CIF ranks tied 3rd of 17 in
classification, behind RF-RFE and XGBoost and level with RF; 2nd of 18 in
regression, behind ExtraTrees. On the smaller panels where every method
completed it ranks tied 6th and tied 5th, and the regression ordering is
descriptive. On synthetic designs with known informative features CIF sits in
the lower half on recovery (8th of 15 and 11th of 16), and it is weakest at
small $k$. Sparse high-$p$ simulations indicate that forest feature sampling can
leave informative features out of many split decisions. At the minimum permutation budget the corrections cost of order
$m^2/\alpha$ permutations per node in the feature test over $m$ features and
$K^2/\alpha$ in the threshold test over $K$ candidate thresholds. A max-type
threshold test, an opt-in alternative, reaches the nominal level with about
$999K$ statistic evaluations, loses 0.006 in power at equal realized size, and
moves CIF to 5th of 17 and 4th of 18. The adaptive stopping rule has exact null
size 0.0488 at its default budget of 999 permutations. Overall, the results
support CIF as a top-$k$ feature-ranking method in the evaluated downstream
prediction benchmarks, level with random forests in classification.
