Conditional inference trees and forests choose a split variable by an
association test before searching for a threshold, which removes the preference
of CART-style splitting for features with many cutpoints. Whether that
correction improves a feature ranking used for downstream prediction, and what
it costs, has not been measured on one footing. We benchmark conditional
inference trees (CIT) and forests (CIF) against 15 classification and 16
regression selectors, among them partykit's ctree and cforest, random forests,
gradient boosting, and permutation importance and wrapper methods, on 31 real
datasets with 5 seeds, scoring every ranking by the held-out accuracy of fixed
learners fitted to its top-$k$ features, with synthetic designs where the
informative features are known. CIF is the strongest conditional inference
ranker in the study: it ranks tied 3rd of 17 in classification and 2nd of 18 in
regression, ahead of cforest and ctree by margins whose bootstrap intervals
exclude zero in classification, level with random forests, behind recursive
feature elimination, and behind boosting on the 13-dataset complete-case panel;
on synthetic recovery it sits in the lower half of the methods, and single
conditional inference trees rank near the bottom. The cost has one source. The
per-threshold Bonferroni threshold test needs a permutation budget of order
$K^2/\alpha$ in the number of candidate thresholds $K$, because at a fixed
budget it cannot reject at all beyond about 50 candidates, and the ablations
place the fitting time there rather than in the feature test, whose adaptive
stopping is inert at the minimum budget. A max-type threshold test resolves the
same level at $999K$ evaluations, is calibrated closer to the nominal level
(Stage B size 0.023 to 0.044 against 0.000 to 0.027 at nominal 0.05), loses
0.006 in power on average at matched size (95 percent interval $-0.009$ to
$-0.004$ over cells, within 0.03 in every cell), and moves the forest from tied
3rd to 5th and from 2nd to 4th; the adaptive stopping rule has null size 0.0488
at a 999-permutation budget, exact under a continuous idealization. For a
practitioner the result is a ranking level with random forests that replaces
CART's high-cardinality preference with a smaller low-cardinality one, at a
fitting cost about two orders of magnitude above a random forest's; an ordinary
random forest ranked by out-of-bag permutation importance removes the same
preference but ranks 8th of 18 and 6th of 19, below CIF, and its permutation
step grows with the number of features; the threshold test, not the feature
test, is where the cost of conditional inference is recovered.
