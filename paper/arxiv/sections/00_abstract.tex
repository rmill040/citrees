Conditional inference trees and forests choose a split variable by an
association test before searching for a threshold, which removes the preference
of CART-style splitting for features with many cutpoints. Whether that
correction improves a feature ranking used for downstream prediction, and what
it costs, has not been measured on one footing. We benchmark conditional
inference trees (CIT) and forests (CIF) against 15 selectors, among them
partykit's ctree and cforest, random forests, gradient boosting, and permutation
importance and wrapper methods, on 31 real datasets with 5 seeds, scoring every
ranking by the held-out accuracy of fixed learners fitted to its top-$k$
features, with synthetic designs where the informative features are known. CIF is the
strongest conditional inference ranker in the study and matches the strongest
CART ensembles: it ranks 3rd of 17 in classification and 2nd of 18 in
regression, ahead of cforest and ctree by margins whose bootstrap intervals
exclude zero in classification, level with random forests and boosting (mean
ranks 5.8 to 6.1 among 17), behind recursive feature elimination, and tied 6th of 17 on the 13-dataset
complete-case panel with every method, learner, and $k$ present, where boosting pulls ahead; single
conditional inference trees rank near the bottom. The cost has one source. The
per-threshold Bonferroni test needs a permutation budget of order $K^2/\alpha$
in the number of candidate thresholds $K$, because at a fixed budget it cannot
reject at all beyond about 50 candidates, and the ablations place the fitting
time there: turning the threshold scan off slows forests by 7.9 to 29 times
while the feature test's adaptive stopping is inert at the minimum budget. A
max-type threshold test resolves the same level at $999K$ evaluations, is
calibrated closer to the nominal level (Stage B size 0.023 to 0.044 against
0.000 to 0.027 at nominal 0.05), shows no detectable difference in power at
matched size, and moves the downstream rankings by at most two positions; the
adaptive stopping rule has null size 0.0488 at its default budget, exact under the continuous idealization the proposition states. For a
practitioner the result is a ranking that matches the best CART ensembles and
is free of their cardinality bias, at a fitting cost about two orders of
magnitude above a random forest's at the companion article's reference condition, and the threshold test, not the feature
test, is where that cost is recovered.
