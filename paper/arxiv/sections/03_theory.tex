\section{Fixed-node Stage~A control}
\label{sec:theory}

This section analyzes the exhaustive fixed-$B$ Stage~A feature selection rule at a
fixed node. The lemma states the standard superuniformity of a single $+1$ Monte Carlo
permutation $p$-value, and the corollary applies Bonferroni correction across the
fixed-node Stage~A tests. In the notation of \cref{sec:stageA-stageB}, this is
the reference case where every feature in $F_t$ is tested with the same fixed
nodewise budget $B$. All probabilities below condition on $(X_t,U)$, where $U$
collects label-independent auxiliary randomness other than the Monte Carlo
permutation draws.

\begin{lemma}[$+1$ permutation $p$-values are superuniform; \citealp{HemerikGoeman2018ExactTestingRandomPermutations}]
\label{lem:plusone-superuniform}
Assume A0.1 through A0.5 from \cref{app:assumptions}. Under the nodewise
complete permutation null at the fixed node $t$, conditional on $(X_t,U)$, the
observed statistic together with the $B$ random-permutation statistics has the
rank distribution of an exchangeable tuple of length $B+1$. Define the
right-tail $+1$ Monte Carlo $p$-value
%
\begin{equation}
  p_{\mathrm{MC}}^{\ge}
  := \frac{1 + \sum_{b=1}^B \ind\{T_b \ge T_{\mathrm{obs}}\}}{B+1}.
\end{equation}
%
Then for every $\alpha \in [0,1]$,
%
\begin{equation}
  \PP(p_{\mathrm{MC}}^{\ge} \le \alpha \mid X_t,U) \le \alpha.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

This is the standard exchangeable-rank result for Monte Carlo permutation tests
\citep{NorthCurtisSham2002EmpiricalPValuesMonteCarlo,Ernst2004PermutationMethodsExactInference,HemerikGoeman2018ExactTestingRandomPermutations}.
\Cref{app:permutation-exchangeability,app:proof-plusone} state the
exchangeability condition and give the proof. The left-tail version follows by
applying the same result to $-T$.

\begin{corollary}[Stage~A complete null control at a fixed node]
\label{cor:stageA-global-null}
Assume A0.1 through A0.5 from \cref{app:assumptions}. Under the nodewise complete
null at $t$, let $F_t$ be nonempty and write $m_t = |F_t| \ge 1$. Let Stage~A
reject when
%
\begin{equation}
  \min_{j \in F_t} p_{t,j} \le \alpha_{\mathrm{sel}}/m_t.
\end{equation}
%
The tree-growing rule uses a strict comparison, so its rejection event is a
subset of the nonstrict event above.
%
Then
%
\begin{equation}
  \PP\!\left(\exists j \in F_t : p_{t,j} \le \alpha_{\mathrm{sel}}/m_t \,\middle|\, X_t,U\right)
  \le \alpha_{\mathrm{sel}}.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

Combining \cref{lem:plusone-superuniform} with the Bonferroni union bound over
the $m_t$ Stage~A tests gives fixed-node Stage~A control under the complete
null. The result is local: it does not cover partial-null familywise error
control, $p$-values for selected features, or error control for fitted trees and
forests.

\begin{corollary}[Stage~B max-type control at a fixed node]
\label{cor:stageB-maxt}
Assume A0.1 through A0.5 from \cref{app:assumptions} at a fixed node $t$, with the tested feature and the finite candidate set $C$ fixed as functions of $(X_t,U)$ alone, a fixed budget $B$ with no optional stopping, and left-tail ties counted with $\le$. Let $p^{\max}_t$ be the max-type Stage~B $p$-value of \cref{eq:maxt-stat} with column moments computed over all $B+1$ rows. Under the nodewise complete permutation null, conditional on $(X_t,U)$, $\PP(p^{\max}_t \le \alpha \mid X_t,U) \le \alpha$ for every $\alpha\in[0,1]$.
\end{corollary}

The argument reduces to \cref{lem:plusone-superuniform} in four steps. Under the complete null the $B+1$ rows of the impurity matrix $(S_{b,c})$ are exchangeable: row $0$ is the observed labels and rows $1,\dots,B$ are independent uniform permutations of them with the identity adjoined \citep{HemerikGoeman2018ExactTestingRandomPermutations}, and \cref{lem:identity-vs-random} applies verbatim to the vector-valued statistic $c \mapsto S_{b,c}$ in $\mathbb{R}^{K}$, since nothing in its proof uses that the statistic is scalar. Column
standardization uses the mean and standard deviation of all $B+1$ entries of
each column, which are symmetric functions of the rows, so the standardized
matrix has exchangeable rows as well; the exclusion of candidates with an empty
child or zero column variance is likewise a function of the whole column. The
row minimum is one fixed function applied to every row, so $T_0,\dots,T_B$ are
exchangeable. The inclusive-rank $p$-value of an exchangeable tuple is
superuniform by the proof of \cref{lem:plusone-superuniform}, applied to $-T$
for the left tail. The moments must include row $0$: estimating them from the permuted rows alone singles out the observed row, breaks the symmetry the argument uses, and can be anti-conservative. The guarantee is weak familywise control over the $K$ candidates, the probability of declaring any split under the node's global null. It is a fixed-node, fixed-$B$ statement for a feature fixed without reference to $Y_t$; it does not cover the adaptive variant, whose column moments and chosen threshold move with the stopping time, and it is not a post-selection $p$-value for a feature chosen by Stage~A from the same responses.

\begin{proposition}[Size of the adaptive stopping rule]
\label{prop:adaptive-size}
Assume A0.1 through A0.3 at a fixed node for one permutation test whose statistic is a fixed function of each permuted response vector (a Stage~A test or a per-threshold Stage~B test), with budget $B$, level $\alpha$, confidence $c$, and batch size $s$. The adaptive rule draws permutations independently and uniformly, with replacement, in batches of $s$; at each batch boundary $m \ge \lceil 1/\alpha \rceil$ it stops when the $\mathrm{Beta}(1+k,\,1+m-k)$ posterior places mass at least $c$ on either side of $\alpha$, where $k$ counts permuted statistics at least as extreme as the observed one, and it reports $(k+1)/(m+1)$. The rule rejects when the reported value is strictly below $\alpha$, as the implementation does. Let $\rho(\alpha,c,s,B)$ be the probability of that event when the same rule is applied to a P\'olya urn started from one ball of each colour. Under the complete nodewise null, conditional on $(X_t,U)$, three statements hold. (i)~Continuous mixing: if the per-draw probability that a permuted statistic is at least as extreme as the observed one is uniform on $(0,1)$, the size equals $\rho$ exactly. (ii)~Finite orbit: for a statistic without ties on the $N = n_t!$ permutations, that probability is uniform on $\{1/N, 2/N, \dots, 1\}$, and the size lies in $[\rho - 1/N,\ \rho]$. (iii)~Ties: counting ties as exceedances can only lower the size, so with ties the size is at most the value in (ii).
At the shipped settings $c=0.95$ and $s=32$, $\rho(0.05,0.95,32,999)=0.0488$, $\rho(0.05,0.95,32,1537)=0.0491$, and $\rho\le 0.0496$ for every $B$ from 20 to 3{,}000 (a numerical statement; the largest value is at $B=140$); $\rho(0.05,0.80,32,999)=0.0522$. At the adjusted levels the tree actually tests, $\rho$ stays below the level: $0.00249$ at $\alpha/20$ with its floor budget of 400 and $0.00196$ at the \texttt{auto} budget of 1{,}533; $0.000195$ at $\alpha/256$ with its floor of 5{,}120 and $0.000153$ at the \texttt{auto} budget. Under the non-strict convention, rejecting when the reported value is at most $\alpha$, the corresponding values are $0.0495$ at $B=999$ and $0.0523$ at $c=0.80$, and the bound $\rho \le \alpha$ fails at $B=79$, where $\rho=0.05001$; the values above are for the strict rule the implementation uses.
\end{proposition}

Write $\theta$ for the per-draw exceedance probability given the observed statistic and the node data. Given $\theta$, the $m$ draws are independent, so the exceedance count after $m$ draws is $\mathrm{Binomial}(m,\theta)$, and the stop and report decisions are deterministic functions of $(m,k)$ at the batch boundaries; the rejection probability $g(\theta)$ is therefore a fixed function of $\theta$, and it is nonincreasing: flipping one indicator from zero to one raises $k$ at every later boundary, so a futility stop (posterior mass at least $c$ above $\alpha$) can only come earlier and a significance stop (mass at least $c$ below $\alpha$) only later, and at a significance stop or at exhaustion the reported value $(k+1)/(m+1)$ is at least as large on the flipped path; the remaining step, that a significance stop always reports a value strictly below $\alpha$, holds at every reachable state of the shipped settings and is checked by the same enumeration. Under the complete null the observed statistic is exchangeable with the $N$ orbit values, so without ties its rank from the top is uniform on $\{1,\dots,N\}$ and $\theta$ is uniform on $\{1/N,\dots,1\}$, which is (ii); the continuous idealization (i) replaces that grid by the uniform distribution on $(0,1)$, under which the binomial mixture is the P\'olya urn started from one ball of each colour, whose next indicator is one with probability $(k+1)/(m+2)$, and $\rho = \int_0^1 g$ by a finite recursion over the stopping states. For nonincreasing $g$ the grid average $N^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{N} g(j/N)$ is at most $\int_0^1 g$ and at least $\int_0^1 g - (g(0)-g(1))/N \ge \rho - 1/N$, which is the interval in (ii). Counting ties as exceedances raises $\theta$ and, given $\theta$, the same nonincreasing $g$ gives (iii). \texttt{paper/theory/adaptive\_stopping\_size.py} regenerates the values of $\rho$, and the accompanying test verifies that the implementation's Beta CDF makes the same stopping decision as a reference implementation at every reachable state.

\begin{remark}
Under the \texttt{minimum} budget with Bonferroni correction the floor $\lceil 1/\alpha_{\mathrm{adj}}\rceil$ equals the budget, so the first stopping check coincides with exhaustion and the rule reduces to the fixed-$B$ test of \cref{lem:plusone-superuniform}. This is the benchmark configuration: every Stage~A and per-threshold Stage~B $p$-value in the benchmark is a fixed-$B$ $+1$ permutation $p$-value. The adaptive max-type kernel recomputes its column moments over all permutations drawn so far at each check, so its exceedance path is not a P\'olya urn and \cref{prop:adaptive-size} does not cover it; its calibration rests on the complete-node simulations.
\end{remark}
