\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Ranking quality}
\label{sec:experiments-real}

With the selected configurations fixed, CIF ranks tied 3rd of 17 in
classification, behind RF-RFE and XGBoost and level with RF, and 2nd of 18 in
regression, behind ExtraTrees, on the all-method panels of 21 and 8 datasets
(\cref{tab:method-main-comparison}). The complete-case Friedman test rejects equal ranks in classification
($\chi^2(16)=140.46$, $P<0.001$, Kendall's $W=0.68$) and does not reject in
regression ($\chi^2(17)=26.15$, $P=0.072$, $W=0.26$), so the regression
ordering is a descriptive summary of 6 datasets. Across datasets, CIF is in
the top half on 17 of 21 classification and 5 of 8 regression datasets, and in
the top 3 on 4 of 21 and 2 of 8.

Against its conditional inference relatives, CIF is ahead of ctree, cforest,
and CIT by both the median and the mean in both tasks
(\cref{tab:conditional-inference-comparisons}). In regression the median is the
primary summary, because $R^2$ is unbounded below. On the three
\nolinkurl{coepra} datasets (76 to 133 observations, about 5,000 features) the
comparators reach large negative $R^2$, and those three datasets set the ctree
and CIT means. On the complete-case panels the classification intervals and
the regression ctree interval exclude zero, and the regression intervals for
cforest and CIT cross it.

The paired bootstrap intervals of CIF against every other method on the
complete-case panels are unadjusted post-hoc contrasts, 16 in classification
and 17 in regression, tabulated with the LODO results and breakdowns by
downstream learner, value of $k$, and seed (\cref{app:benchmark-stability}). In
classification they favor RF-RFE, XGBoost, and LightGBM against CIF and include
zero for RF, ExtraTrees, CatBoost, and Boruta, so the data do not order CIF
among these four, and in regression no interval favors another method over
CIF. The complete-case panels rank CIF lower, tied 6th of 17 and tied 5th of 18
(\cref{tab:method-main-comparison}). LODO reselection moves it to 8th and 6th there,
reselecting the benchmark configuration in 11 of 13 and 4 of 6 holdouts, and on
the all-method panels to 5th in classification and 2nd in regression,
reselecting it in 20 of 21 and 7 of 8. The 3rd and 2nd places are therefore
properties of the 21- and 8-dataset panels, and configuration reselection moves
them by less than half a rank.

Two checks address the importance measure. Ranked by cforest's own out-of-bag
permutation importance, CIF leads cforest in classification by $+0.012$ mean
and $+0.006$ median balanced accuracy, with 15 wins in 20 datasets, against
$+0.011$ for split-ranked CIF, so the classification margin persists under a
common importance mechanism. The standard remedies for the cardinality bias of
impurity importance rank below the random forest itself. Ranked by the same
out-of-bag permutation importance (RF permutation in
\cref{tab:conditional-inference-comparisons}), the benchmark's 100-tree random
forest places 8th of 18 in classification and 6th of 19 in regression, against
tied 3rd and 5th for its impurity importance, and cforest, permutation
importance, and conditional permutation importance place 9th, 12th, and 17th of
17 in classification and 7th, 16th, and 18th of 18 in regression
(\cref{tab:method-main-comparison}). With RF permutation present, CIF ranks 4th
of 18 in classification and stays 2nd in regression
(\cref{app:benchmark-stability}).
