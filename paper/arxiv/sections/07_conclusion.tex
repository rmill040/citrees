\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

On 31 real datasets, CIF ranks tied 3rd of 17 in classification, behind RF-RFE
and XGBoost and level with RF, and 2nd of 18 in regression, behind ExtraTrees.
On the complete-case panels it is tied 6th of 17 and tied 5th of 18, and the
regression ordering is descriptive. It is weaker at identifying the informative
features themselves, at or below the median on synthetic recovery (8th of 15
and 11th of 16), and its rankings are weakest at small $k$ in classification
and on wide data.

The two corrections set what a fit costs: under the minimum budget the Stage~A
feature test needs of order $m_t^2/\alpha$ permutations per node over $m_t$
features, and the per-threshold rule of order $K^2/\alpha$ over $K$ candidate
thresholds. The opt-in max-type rule tests the candidates jointly at the
nominal level with about $999K$ statistic evaluations, loses 0.006 in power at
equal realized size, and moves CIF to 5th of 17 and 4th of 18, so it lowers the
cost without improving the ranking. As a top-$k$ feature ranker for downstream
prediction, CIF is level with random forests in classification and ahead of
every other conditional inference ranker in both tasks.
