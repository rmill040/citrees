\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The question was how conditional inference trees and forests compare with
established feature rankers, and what the Bonferroni corrections in their split
tests cost and change. On 31 real datasets CIF is the strongest conditional
inference ranker in the study, ahead of cforest, ctree, and single CIT. CIF
ranks tied 3rd of 17 in classification, behind RF-RFE and XGBoost and level with
RF; 2nd of 18 in regression, behind ExtraTrees. On the complete-case panels it
is tied 6th of 17 and tied 5th of 18, and the regression ordering is
descriptive; with the out-of-bag permutation forest added it is 4th of 18 in
classification. The negative answers are as clear. On the synthetic designs CIF
sits in the lower half on recovery of the informative features (8th of 15 and
11th of 16), it is weakest at small $k$, and in sparse high-dimensional simulations
forest feature sampling keeps informative features out of most splits. The
corrections change which features are ranked more than how well the rankings
predict: on the weak-signal designs, run with the linear selectors, the
per-threshold rule trades the high-cardinality preference of CART-style
splitting for a low-cardinality preference of comparable size, and the random
forest ranked by out-of-bag permutation importance keeps the high-cardinality
preference there and ranks below CIF on the benchmark (8th of 18 and 6th of
19), with a permutation step that grows with the number of features.

The cost of the corrections has two terms. Under the minimum budget the Stage~A
feature test needs of order $m_t^2/\alpha$ permutations per node and the
per-threshold rule of the Stage~B threshold test of order $K^2/\alpha$, which it
needs in order to reject at all; the threshold term dominates on most datasets
and the feature term on the widest. The max-type rule is an opt-in alternative
that reaches the nominal level with about $999K$ evaluations, has realized size
closer to nominal, loses about 0.006 in power at equal realized size, and moves
CIF to 5th of 17 and 4th of 18; its fixed-node guarantee covers a fixed budget
and a feature fixed without the response, and its calibration under adaptive
stopping in fitted trees rests on the simulations. Each split test is
calibrated at a fixed node conditional on the node sample, for subsampled or
unsampled fits; the benchmarked forests draw bootstrap samples, and their
rankings are assessed empirically. For a practitioner choosing a ranking method
for downstream prediction, CIF delivers a ranking level with random forests in
classification, and the max-type rule lowers its cost at nodes with many
candidate thresholds.
