\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The question was whether the split-selection correction of conditional
inference improves a feature ranking used for downstream prediction, and what
it costs. On 31 real datasets CIF is the best conditional inference ranker in the
study, ahead of cforest, ctree, and single CIT, and it ranks tied 3rd of 17 and
2nd of 18 overall, level with random forest rankings, behind recursive feature
elimination and, on the 13-dataset complete-case panel, behind boosting; the
max-type configuration recommended below ranks 5th and 4th. The correction
replaces the high-cardinality preference of CART-style splitting, which the
NHANES application of the companion article shows on real clinical predictors,
with a smaller low-cardinality one under the per-threshold rule; on these
benchmarks it shows in which features are ranked, with top-$k$ scores level
with random forests and below impurity importance in the recovery of weak
signals, and out-of-bag permutation importance removes the high-cardinality
preference from an ordinary random forest without improving its ranking (8th
of 18 and 6th of 19, below CIF) and at a permutation cost that grows with the
number of features.

The answer to the second part is precise. The cost of conditional inference is
the per-threshold Stage~B test, whose permutation budget must grow as
$K^2/\alpha$ in the number of candidate thresholds for the rule to reject at
all; the feature test and its adaptive stopping are not the cost at the
benchmark's budget. The max-type threshold test resolves the same level at
$999K$ evaluations, is calibrated closer to the nominal level, loses about
0.006 in power at matched size, and moves the downstream rankings by at most
two positions, so it is the configuration a user who wants conditional
inference at a practical cost should choose; its fixed-node guarantee covers
the fixed-budget test, and its calibration under adaptive stopping rests on the
simulations. For a practitioner choosing a ranking method for downstream
prediction, conditional inference delivers a ranking level with the best
ensembles whose split decisions carry a calibrated error rate; its cost is the
threshold test, and the max-type construction is where that budget is
recovered.
